## 1. Brand Positioning (so the design *feels* right, not just looks right)

**What Merchantiva is:** The decision-and-implementation partner for ecommerce entrepreneurs. Merchantiva helps entrepreneurs figure out what to do, then helps them do it — across the entire lifecycle of building an ecommerce business, from first idea to eventual sale.

**What Merchantiva is NOT:** A generic ecommerce agency. A dropshipping course. A marketing-influencer brand. A freelancer marketplace. A research-report shop (research is a capability that informs decisions and implementation — never sold or presented as the standalone thing customers are buying).

**Brand personality:** Strategic, Intelligent, Research-driven, Practical, Trustworthy, Modern, Entrepreneurial. Should feel like a strategy firm or research/technology company — not like a shopping/retail brand.

**Guiding principle / tagline:** "Better decisions. Better businesses." Core question customers are implicitly asking on every page: *"What is the next best decision for this ecommerce entrepreneur?"*

**Voice:** Direct, credible, research-grounded — not hype-y, not "guru" marketing language. Speaks to entrepreneurs as capable adults making real business decisions. Confident but not overpromising.

## 2. Visual Identity System

**Colors:**
| Role | Name | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Deep Navy | `#0B0F14` | Main backgrounds, brand foundation |
| Secondary | Dark Slate | `#2A3441` | Cards, UI components, secondary backgrounds |
| Neutral | Cool Gray | `#667182` | Supporting text, secondary information |
| Accent | Electric Cyan | `#4DD6FF` | CTAs, highlights, interactive elements, data viz |
| Off-white | — | `#F5F7FA` | Light backgrounds, light-mode surfaces |

**Typography:** Clean sans-serif, strong hierarchy, high readability, professional/technical feel — not decorative. Headlines can use a serif weight for gravitas (see logo treatment below) but body/UI text should stay sans-serif.

**Logo:** Bold "M" monogram with a small negative-space diamond accent in the counter/notch — represents a decision point/moment of clarity. The diamond only reads correctly against a light background (it's negative space, not a separate colored shape). Full lockup includes the wordmark "MERCHANTIVA" (tracked-out caps) and tagline "BETTER DECISIONS. BETTER BUSINESSES." beneath. Use the full lockup in the header; a simplified bold standalone version of the M for favicon/small contexts.

**Imagery direction:** Entrepreneurs building companies, data/analysis, strategy sessions, ecommerce operations, technology systems. Avoid: shopping carts, product shelves, shipping boxes, generic retail/consumer shopping imagery, discount-oriented visuals, ships/maritime imagery, ornamental/heraldic crests.

**Design principle:** Every visual element should reinforce — Merchantiva transforms ecommerce uncertainty into informed decisions.

## 3. Full Sitemap — Design Every Page Below

### Navigation (persistent across all pages)
Lifecycle · Services · Resources · Academy · About · Contact — with a persistent "Start Here" CTA button.

### Page 1 — Home (`/`)
- Hero: one-line strategic position + subhead explaining the lifecycle model. Headline should communicate "we'll help you figure it out and help you build it" — never "buy a research report."
- "Where are you in your journey?" — a grid-of-cards selector mapping to the 7 lifecycle stages (Discover, Validate, Launch, Acquire Customers, Scale, Expand, Exit), each card linking to its section on the Lifecycle page
- Brief explainer of the four connected engines: Intelligence, Education (Academy), Implementation, Growth
- Social proof section (use `[PLACEHOLDER]` for testimonials/logos — do not invent names or numbers)
- Free content teaser (2–3 article/resource cards)
- Primary CTA: email/newsletter signup
- Secondary CTA: explore services (leads to the 4 commercial offers, not to research/reports)

**SEO intent:** Entry point for "ecommerce business help," "how to start an ecommerce business," "ecommerce decision partner." Primary heading should contain the core value prop in plain language, not jargon.

### Page 2 — Lifecycle (`/lifecycle`)
One page, all 7 stages as sections with jump-to anchors (not 7 separate pages). For each stage include: stage name + goal, the customer questions it answers, services offered at that stage, and a stage-specific CTA framed as an outcome (e.g. Discover → "Find out what to sell — and get help building it," not "Get an opportunity report").

**The 7 stages (goal + customer questions to display):**
1. **Discover** — Find an opportunity worth pursuing. *"What should I sell? Which niche? Where do I find suppliers? Is this worth it?"*
2. **Validate** — Determine if it can be a real business. *"Is there demand? Who are my customers? How do I price? What makes me different?"*
3. **Launch** — Launch the store. *"How do I build it? Which platform? Payments? What goes on the site?"*
4. **Acquire Customers** — Generate consistent sales. *"How do I get customers? Which channels? Better conversions?"*
5. **Scale** — Build a bigger, more efficient business. *"How do I automate? Improve ops? Increase profit?"*
6. **Expand** — Grow beyond the original business. *"What's next? New markets? Stronger brand?"*
7. **Exit** — Maximize value, prepare for sale. *"What's it worth? How do I prep for acquisition? Where do I find buyers?"*

**SEO intent:** Each stage's anchor section should be able to independently rank for its underlying question (e.g. "what should I sell online," "how to validate an ecommerce idea," "how to launch a Shopify store," "ecommerce exit planning"). Use the customer questions above as natural H2/H3 language, not as a hidden keyword list.

### Page 3 — Services (`/services`)
Lead with 4 commercial offers, headlined by outcome, not method. Method-level detail (research, Shopify setup, SEO, automation) is supporting detail underneath each headline — never the headline or nav label itself.

1. **Start an ecommerce business** — "Go from idea to a store that's actually live." *(research opportunities → recommend a direction → develop the concept → build the store → set up technical infrastructure → prepare for launch)*
2. **Launch a store** — "A store built (or rebuilt) to convert." *(product pages, branding, analytics, email, technical integrations, conversion foundations)*
3. **Get customers** — "Turn traffic into a growth engine — and keep improving it." *(research customers/competitors/channels → acquisition strategy → paid ads/SEO/content/email/influencers/conversion → measure → optimize)*
4. **Scale operations** — "Stop doing repetitive marketing work by hand." *(identify repetitive processes → automation → AI workflows, CRM, email automation, reporting, lifecycle systems)*

Below the four offers, a lighter-weight "how we work" section covering the broader capability system (Free Content, Academy, Intelligence, Implementation, Growth & Automation, Exit) — visually secondary to the four commercial offers, not four more things to buy.

**SEO intent:** "ecommerce store development," "ecommerce growth agency," "ecommerce marketing automation" — but the page copy itself should stay outcome-first; keyword-relevant terms appear naturally in supporting detail, not forced into headlines.

### Page 4 — Resources (`/resources`)
- Blog/article index, filterable or tagged by lifecycle stage
- Prominent newsletter signup
- Space for downloadable lead magnets (opportunity reports, launch checklists, supplier directories, planning templates) — use `[PLACEHOLDER]` titles if none are finalized

**SEO intent:** This is the primary organic-content engine — long-tail queries mapped to each lifecycle stage's customer questions (see Page 2). Design should support frequent new article cards without looking sparse when the library is small at launch.

### Page 5 — Academy (`/academy`)
- What's included: courses, tutorials, guides, templates, checklists, workshops, office hours, research library
- Positioned as the education/recurring-relationship layer — not the primary revenue focus, but should look like a real, substantial offering
- Pricing: `[PLACEHOLDER]` if not finalized — do not invent numbers
- CTA: join / waitlist

**SEO intent:** "ecommerce course," "learn ecommerce," secondary to the four commercial offers in priority but still a real landing destination.

### Page 6 — About (`/about`)
- Mission, vision, guiding principle — pulled directly from the positioning above, not rewritten
- Why needs-based positioning / why Merchantiva isn't a course platform or agency

**SEO intent:** Brand/trust queries ("who is Merchantiva," "Merchantiva reviews") — design should support future testimonial/credibility content once it exists.

### Page 7 — Contact / Start Here (`/contact` or `/start`)
- Simple way for a visitor to indicate their current lifecycle stage and what they need
- Email capture at minimum

**SEO intent:** Conversion page, not a discovery page — design for clarity and low friction over keyword targeting.
