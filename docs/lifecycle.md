# The Ecommerce Entrepreneur Lifecycle

## Primary Framework: Five Customer-Facing Stages
Merchantiva's website, navigation, customer segmentation, JTBD structure, service organization, product roadmap, content strategy, and positioning are organized around **five** stages — plain language an entrepreneur recognizes immediately, without needing to know Porter, Christensen, HBR, or any business-growth theory behind it.

**Start Your Business → Get Your First Sales → Build a Profitable Business → Grow Your Business → Sell Your Business**

The test for this framework: an entrepreneur should look at the five stages and immediately think *"that's where I am"* — not need the underlying theory explained to identify their stage.

---

### Stage 1 — Start Your Business

**Business situation:** The entrepreneur has an idea, interest, or intention to start an ecommerce business but does not yet have a functioning business with established customer demand. They are trying to turn an idea into a viable ecommerce business.

**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me figure out what to sell and get my ecommerce business started."*

**Progress:**
- **FROM:** An idea, interest, or intention to start an ecommerce business.
- **TO:** A real ecommerce business that is ready to sell.

**Customer questions and jobs:**
What should I sell? What market should I enter? Is there a real opportunity here? Who is the customer? What problem am I solving? What products should I offer? How large is the opportunity? Who are my competitors? What are competitors doing? Where can I source the product? How much will the product cost? What could my margins look like? Can I validate demand before investing heavily? What should my offer be? How should I position the business? What should I call the business? What should my store look like? How do I set up Shopify? How do I accept payments? What analytics do I need? What email systems do I need? What marketing systems do I need? What do I need to do before launching?

**Merchantiva needs:** Opportunity discovery, product research, market research, customer research, competitor research, supplier research, product validation, business model decisions, positioning, offer development, brand decisions, store setup, ecommerce technology, analytics, basic marketing infrastructure, launch planning.

**Merchantiva services:** Ecommerce Opportunity Reports, Product Research, Market/Niche Research, Competitor Research, Supplier Research, Product Validation, Ecommerce Validation Packages, Positioning Strategy, Offer Development, Brand/Business Naming, Shopify Store Setup, Ecommerce Launch Packages, Payment Setup, Analytics Setup, Email Setup, Basic Automation, Launch Planning.

**Stage transition:** The business is launched and ready to sell, and the entrepreneur's primary problem shifts from "How do I start?" to "How do I get customers?"

---

### Stage 2 — Get Your First Sales

**Business situation:** The ecommerce business exists and is capable of accepting orders, but customer demand has not yet been demonstrated consistently. The entrepreneur's biggest question is no longer whether they can launch — it's whether they can actually attract customers and generate sales.

**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me get customers and prove people will buy."*

**Progress:**
- **FROM:** A launched ecommerce business with an unproven market.
- **TO:** A business with real customers and evidence of demand.

**Customer questions and jobs:**
How do I get my first customers? Where can I find potential customers? Which marketing channel should I use? Should I use paid advertising? Should I use SEO? Should I create content? How do I use email marketing? Why aren't people buying? Is my offer compelling? Is my pricing right? Is my website converting? Are customers finding what they need? What is preventing people from purchasing? How much does it cost to acquire a customer? Which acquisition channels actually work? Are customers coming back? How do I get repeat purchases? Is there enough demand to continue investing in this business?

**Merchantiva needs:** Customer acquisition, marketing, conversion optimization, pricing, offer optimization, customer retention, analytics, demand measurement, channel testing, unit economics, early profitability analysis.

**Merchantiva services:** Customer Acquisition Strategy, Paid Advertising, SEO, Content Marketing, Email Marketing, Conversion Rate Optimization, Pricing Analysis, Offer Optimization, Customer Retention Programs, Marketing Analytics, Customer Acquisition Analysis, Ecommerce Growth Audits, Profitability Analysis.

**Stage transition:** The business has demonstrated that customers will buy and there is enough evidence to believe the business can become economically viable. The key change: *"Can I get people to buy?"* → *"How do I make this business work consistently?"*

---

### Stage 3 — Build a Profitable Business

**Business situation:** The entrepreneur has demonstrated demand. The business is no longer simply an experiment. Now the challenge is building a stable, repeatable, profitable operation — understanding and improving the economics and systems behind the business.

**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me make the business consistently profitable and build the systems to support it."*

**Progress:**
- **FROM:** A business that has demonstrated customer demand.
- **TO:** A stable, profitable business with repeatable systems.

**Customer questions and jobs:**
How do I improve profitability? Are my margins good enough? Where am I losing money? How do I improve customer lifetime value? How do I increase repeat purchases? How do I improve operations? How should I manage inventory? How should I manage customer support? What should I automate? What should I delegate? What systems do I need? What processes should I document? How do I create repeatable processes? How do I manage data and analytics? What technology should I use? How do I reduce errors? How do I reduce my dependence on myself? Should I add more products? Which products should I add? How do I make the business more predictable?

**Merchantiva needs:** Profitability, operations, automation, analytics, customer management, inventory management, process development, technology, delegation, product expansion, repeatability.

**Merchantiva services:** Growth Audits, Profitability Analysis, Ecommerce Analytics, CRM Systems, Inventory Systems, Customer Support Systems, Marketing Automation, AI Automation, Operations Automation, SOP Development, Ecommerce Operations Systems, Product Expansion Strategy, Customer Retention Systems, Technology/System Integration.

**Stage transition:** The business has a reliable economic model and repeatable systems, and the owner's primary challenge becomes managing and sustaining significant growth. The key change: *"How do I make this work?"* → *"How do I make it bigger?"*

---

### Stage 4 — Grow Your Business

**Business situation:** The business has proven its model and established systems. The entrepreneur now wants to grow substantially. Growth creates a new set of problems: more customers, more products, more employees, more operations, more capital requirements, and more organizational complexity. The challenge is not simply generating growth — it's **growing without losing control of the business.**

**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me grow the business without losing control of it."*

**Progress:**
- **FROM:** A stable and profitable ecommerce business.
- **TO:** A larger, more capable, and more valuable company.

**Customer questions and jobs:**
How do I scale customer acquisition? Which channels can support larger growth? How do I expand my product line? Should I enter new markets? Should I expand internationally? How do I build a team? Who should I hire? What should I delegate? What should I automate? How do I increase operational capacity? How do I manage increasing complexity? How do I maintain profitability while growing? How do I improve forecasting? How do I manage more inventory? How do I build better management systems? How do I reduce risk? How do I make the company less dependent on me? How do I increase the value of the company?

**Merchantiva needs:** Growth strategy, advanced customer acquisition, product expansion, market expansion, team building, management systems, advanced automation, operational scaling, partnerships, strategic planning, risk management, business value creation.

**Merchantiva services:** Advanced Growth Strategy, Marketing Scaling, Product Expansion, Market Expansion, International Expansion, Partnership Strategy, Team/Organizational Systems, Advanced Automation, Operational Scaling, Strategic Planning, Business Value Optimization.

**Stage transition:** The business has become a substantial asset and the owner's priorities shift from simply growing the company toward protecting, optimizing, and ultimately realizing the value they have created. The key change: *"How do I grow this?"* → *"How do I maximize what I've built?"*

---

### Stage 5 — Sell Your Business

**Business situation:** The entrepreneur has built a meaningful ecommerce business and now wants to maximize the value of the asset they have created. Selling is not necessarily the only possible outcome, but it represents the point at which the entrepreneur begins thinking about value realization and strategic options.

**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me maximize the value of what I've built."*

**Progress:**
- **FROM:** Owning and operating a valuable ecommerce business.
- **TO:** A business that is prepared for a strategic transaction or other form of value realization.

**Customer questions and jobs:**
What is my business worth? What do buyers look for? How do I make the business more attractive to buyers? How do I make the business less dependent on me? How do I strengthen management? How do I improve systems? How do I reduce risk? How do I prepare my financials? What information will buyers want? How do I prepare for due diligence? Where can I find potential buyers? Should I sell now or continue growing? How do I maximize the sale price? How do I prepare the company for acquisition? How do I structure the transaction? What should I do after the sale?

**Merchantiva needs:** Value creation, risk reduction, management systems, owner independence, financial preparation, acquisition readiness, valuation preparation, buyer research, transaction preparation, exit strategy.

**Merchantiva services:** Acquisition Readiness, Business Value Analysis, Exit Planning, Valuation Preparation, Buyer Research, Due Diligence Preparation, Management/System Readiness, Risk Reduction, Transaction Preparation, Strategic Exit Services.

**Stage transition:** This stage does not necessarily have a single next stage. The entrepreneur may sell the business, continue operating it, bring in a partner, acquire another business, reinvest in growth, or start another company. The important strategic concept: the business has become an asset whose value can be strategically realized.

## Internal Operating Resolution: Seven Detailed Stages
Kept as internal decomposition — a source of preserved jobs and activities, **not a competing lifecycle architecture**. The seven stages don't map one-to-one into the five; they're reorganized thematically underneath them. Don't debate individual stage-name placement (e.g. "does Scale belong under Build or Grow") — that's the wrong question. The right question is which broader customer situation each *job* now belongs to.

| New stage | What it absorbs from the old model |
|---|---|
| Start Your Business | Discover + Validate + Launch |
| Get Your First Sales | Acquire |
| Build a Profitable Business | Profitability + operations + systems + automation + retention (a cross-cutting theme, not one old stage — conversion, retention, inventory, analytics, automation, CRM, SOPs, delegation, reducing owner dependence) |
| Grow Your Business | Scale + Expand *(both, together — not split between stages)* |
| Sell Your Business | Exit |

**The critical distinction driving this split:** Build = make the business work. Grow = make the working business bigger. That's what makes the two stages meaningfully different from each other — not which old stage name historically covered which topic.

The seven sections below are kept as reference for the specific jobs/services each old stage contained — useful when writing detailed content — but none of them is "owned" by exactly one new stage. Read them as a pool of preserved detail, not a second navigation structure.

### Discover
**Goal:** Discover an ecommerce opportunity worth pursuing.
**Customer questions:** What should I sell? Which niche should I enter? Where can I find suppliers? Is this opportunity worth pursuing?
**Services:** Product research, niche research, market research, trend analysis, competitor research, supplier research, opportunity reports, product validation.

### Validate
**Goal:** Determine whether the opportunity can become a successful business.
**Customer questions:** Is there enough demand? Who are my customers? How should I price my products? What makes me different?
**Services:** Customer research, pricing analysis, competitive positioning, product-market fit analysis, brand positioning, business model validation, launch planning.

### Launch
**Goal:** Launch an ecommerce business.
**Customer questions:** How do I build my store? Which platform should I use? How do I accept payments? What should my website include?
**Services:** Shopify store setup, product listings, landing pages, analytics setup, email setup, basic automation, conversion optimization.

### Acquire Customers
**Goal:** Generate consistent sales.
**Customer questions:** How do I get customers? Which marketing channels should I use? How can I improve conversions?
**Services:** SEO strategy, content marketing, email marketing, paid advertising strategy, influencer research, funnel optimization, conversion optimization.

### Scale
**Goal:** Build a larger and more efficient business.
**Customer questions:** How do I automate my business? How do I improve operations? How do I increase profitability?
**Services:** Workflow automation, CRM implementation, analytics dashboards, inventory systems, customer support systems, AI automation, standard operating procedures.

### Expand
**Goal:** Grow beyond the original business.
**Customer questions:** What should I launch next? Should I enter new markets? How do I strengthen my brand?
**Services:** Product expansion research, market expansion, brand strategy, partnership strategy, growth planning.

### Exit
**Goal:** Maximize business value and prepare for sale.
**Customer questions:** What is my business worth? How do I prepare for acquisition? Where do I find buyers?
**Services:** Exit planning, business valuation preparation, buyer research, sale preparation.

## Relationship to the HBR "Five Stages of Small Business Growth" Framework
Churchill & Lewis's HBR framework (Existence → Survival → Success → Take-off → Resource Maturity) is **intellectual input, not Merchantiva's customer-facing framework.** It validates *why* a stage boundary is real — a meaningful transition involves a real change in the owner's primary objective, the business's economics, required capabilities, organizational complexity, and the owner's role — not just "we offer a different service now." It answers *"what fundamentally changes as a small business grows?"* Merchantiva's five stages answer a related but different question: *"what does an ecommerce entrepreneur need to accomplish next?"* Do not use HBR's terminology (Existence, Survival, Take-off, Resource Maturity, etc.) as customer-facing language — it stays behind the scenes, informing stage boundaries, never appearing in nav, copy, or content.

## Superseded: The Four-Stage Version
An earlier revision of this doc used a four-stage customer-facing framework (Start → Launch → Grow → Exit). That version is superseded by the five-stage version above — "Launch" is absorbed into "Start Your Business," and "Get Your First Sales" is elevated to its own stage rather than being folded into "Grow." Kept here only so anyone who built against the four-stage version understands what changed; do not build new work against it.

---

## Why This Structure
The test: an entrepreneur should look at the five stages and immediately identify where they are — without needing to understand HBR, Porter, Jobs To Be Done, or business-growth theory. Those frameworks inform the strategy behind the scenes. The five-stage version is the customer-facing resolution; the seven-stage version is the internal operating resolution used to design what actually happens *within* each stage. Neither replaces the other — see `docs/venture-operating-system.md` for how this relates to the separate internal *production* pipeline (what Merchantiva's team does, as opposed to what the customer needs).
