# The Ecommerce Entrepreneur Lifecycle

## Primary Framework: Five Customer-Facing Stages
Merchantiva's website, navigation, customer segmentation, JTBD structure, service organization, product roadmap, content strategy, and positioning are organized around **five** stages — plain language an entrepreneur recognizes immediately, without needing to know Porter, Christensen, HBR, or any business-growth theory behind it.

**Start Your Business → Get Your First Sales → Build a Profitable Business → Grow Your Business → Sell Your Business**

The test for this framework: an entrepreneur should look at the five stages and immediately think *"that's where I am"* — not need the underlying theory explained to identify their stage.

### Stage 1 — Start Your Business
**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me figure out what to sell and get my ecommerce business started."*
**Business situation:** Has an idea, interest, or intention to start an ecommerce business but no functioning business with established demand yet.
**Progress:** An idea/intention → A real ecommerce business that is ready to sell.
**Customer questions:** What should I sell? What market should I enter? Is there a real opportunity? Who is the customer? Who are competitors? Where do I source the product? What are my margins? Can I validate demand before investing heavily? How should I position the business? What should my store look like? How do I set up Shopify, payments, analytics, email? What do I need before launching?
**Capabilities:** Opportunity discovery, product/market/customer/competitor/supplier research, product validation, business-model decisions, positioning, offer development, brand decisions, store setup, ecommerce technology, analytics, basic marketing infrastructure, launch planning.
**Stage transition:** The business is launched and ready to sell, and the primary problem shifts from "How do I start?" to "How do I get customers?"

### Stage 2 — Get Your First Sales
**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me get customers and prove people will buy."*
**Business situation:** The store exists and can accept orders, but demand hasn't been demonstrated consistently yet.
**Progress:** A launched business with an unproven market → A business with real customers and evidence of demand.
**Customer questions:** How do I get my first customers? Which channel should I use — paid, SEO, content, email? Why aren't people buying? Is my offer/pricing right? Is my site converting? What's my customer acquisition cost? Which channels actually work? Are customers coming back? Is there enough demand to keep investing?
**Capabilities:** Customer acquisition, marketing, conversion optimization, pricing, offer optimization, customer retention, analytics, demand measurement, channel testing, unit economics, early profitability analysis.
**Stage transition:** The business has demonstrated customers will buy, and there's enough evidence to believe it can become economically viable. The question shifts from "Can I get people to buy?" to "How do I make this work consistently?"

### Stage 3 — Build a Profitable Business
**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me make the business consistently profitable and build the systems to support it."*
**Business situation:** Demand is demonstrated; this is no longer an experiment. The challenge is building a stable, repeatable, profitable operation.
**Progress:** A business with demonstrated demand → A stable, profitable business with repeatable systems.
**Customer questions:** How do I improve profitability/margins? Where am I losing money? How do I improve LTV and repeat purchases? How do I improve operations, inventory, support? What should I automate or delegate? What systems/processes do I need? How do I reduce my dependence on myself? Should I add more products?
**Capabilities:** Profitability analysis, operations, automation, analytics, customer management, inventory management, process development, technology, delegation support, product expansion, repeatability.
**Stage transition:** The business has a reliable economic model and repeatable systems, and the owner's primary challenge becomes managing and sustaining significant growth. The question shifts from "How do I make this work?" to "How do I make it bigger?"

### Stage 4 — Grow Your Business
**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me grow the business without losing control of it."*
**Business situation:** The model and systems are proven; the entrepreneur wants substantial growth. Growth creates new complexity — more customers, products, employees, operations, capital, org complexity.
**Progress:** A stable, profitable business → A larger, more capable, more valuable company.
**Customer questions:** How do I scale acquisition? Should I expand product lines, markets, internationally? How do I build a team — who to hire, what to delegate, what to automate? How do I manage increasing complexity while maintaining profitability? How do I make the company less dependent on me? How do I increase its value?
**Capabilities:** Growth strategy, advanced customer acquisition, product/market expansion, team building, management systems, advanced automation, operational scaling, partnerships, strategic planning, risk management, business value creation.
**Stage transition:** The business has become a substantial asset, and priorities shift from growing it toward protecting, optimizing, and eventually realizing the value created. The question shifts from "How do I grow this?" to "How do I maximize what I've built?"

### Stage 5 — Sell Your Business
**Primary JTBD:** *"Help me maximize the value of what I've built."*
**Business situation:** A meaningful business exists; the entrepreneur wants to maximize its value. Selling isn't the only possible outcome — this stage is about value realization and strategic options broadly.
**Progress:** Owning and operating a valuable business → A business prepared for a strategic transaction or other value realization.
**Customer questions:** What is my business worth? What do buyers look for? How do I reduce risk, strengthen management, prepare financials? How do I prepare for due diligence? Where do I find buyers? Should I sell now or keep growing? How do I maximize sale price and prepare for acquisition?
**Capabilities:** Value creation, risk reduction, management systems, owner independence, financial preparation, acquisition readiness, valuation preparation, buyer research, transaction preparation, exit strategy.
**Stage transition:** No single next stage — the entrepreneur may sell, continue operating, bring in a partner, acquire another business, reinvest in growth, or start another company. The key point: the business has become an asset whose value can be strategically realized.

**Use these five stages for:** website architecture, website navigation, customer segmentation, Jobs To Be Done structure (`docs/jobs-to-be-done.md`), service organization, product roadmap, content strategy, lead generation, sales conversations, customer journeys, positioning (`docs/positioning.md`), future technology/product development.

## Internal Operating Resolution: Seven Detailed Stages
Kept as internal decomposition — used when designing specific offers, services, content, research, or workflows, not as customer-facing navigation or messaging.

| New stage | Detailed stages that belong here |
|---|---|
| Start Your Business | Discover, Validate, Launch |
| Get Your First Sales | Acquire |
| Build a Profitable Business | Early Scale, Optimization, Operations |
| Grow Your Business | Scale, Expand |
| Sell Your Business | Exit |

### Discover
**Goal:** Discover an ecommerce opportunity worth pursuing.
**Customer questions:** What should I sell? Which niche should I enter? Where can I find suppliers? Is this opportunity worth pursuing?
**Services:** Product research, niche research, market research, trend analysis, competitor research, supplier research, opportunity reports, product validation.

### Validate
**Goal:** Determine whether the opportunity can become a successful business.
**Customer questions:** Is there enough demand? Who are my customers? How should I price my products? What makes me different?
**Services:** Customer research, pricing analysis, competitive positioning, product-market fit analysis, brand positioning, business model validation, launch planning.

### Launch
**Goal:** Launch an ecommerce business.
**Customer questions:** How do I build my store? Which platform should I use? How do I accept payments? What should my website include?
**Services:** Shopify store setup, product listings, landing pages, analytics setup, email setup, basic automation, conversion optimization.

### Acquire Customers
**Goal:** Generate consistent sales.
**Customer questions:** How do I get customers? Which marketing channels should I use? How can I improve conversions?
**Services:** SEO strategy, content marketing, email marketing, paid advertising strategy, influencer research, funnel optimization, conversion optimization.

### Scale
**Goal:** Build a larger and more efficient business.
**Customer questions:** How do I automate my business? How do I improve operations? How do I increase profitability?
**Services:** Workflow automation, CRM implementation, analytics dashboards, inventory systems, customer support systems, AI automation, standard operating procedures.

### Expand
**Goal:** Grow beyond the original business.
**Customer questions:** What should I launch next? Should I enter new markets? How do I strengthen my brand?
**Services:** Product expansion research, market expansion, brand strategy, partnership strategy, growth planning.

### Exit
**Goal:** Maximize business value and prepare for sale.
**Customer questions:** What is my business worth? How do I prepare for acquisition? Where do I find buyers?
**Services:** Exit planning, business valuation preparation, buyer research, sale preparation.

## Relationship to the HBR "Five Stages of Small Business Growth" Framework
Churchill & Lewis's HBR framework (Existence → Survival → Success → Take-off → Resource Maturity) is **intellectual input, not Merchantiva's customer-facing framework.** It's used to validate *why* a stage boundary is real — a meaningful transition involves a real change in the owner's primary objective, the business's economics, required capabilities, organizational complexity, and the owner's role — not just "we offer a different service now." It answers *"what fundamentally changes as a small business grows?"* Merchantiva's five stages answer a related but different question: *"what does an ecommerce entrepreneur need to accomplish next?"* Do not use HBR's terminology (Existence, Survival, Take-off, Resource Maturity, etc.) as customer-facing language — it stays behind the scenes, informing stage boundaries, never appearing in nav, copy, or content.

## Superseded: The Four-Stage Version
An earlier revision of this doc used a four-stage customer-facing framework (Start → Launch → Grow → Exit). That version is now superseded by the five-stage version above — "Launch" is absorbed into "Start Your Business," and "Get Your First Sales" is elevated to its own stage rather than being folded into "Grow." Kept here only so anyone who built against the four-stage version understands what changed and why; do not build new work against it.

## Why This Structure
A visitor should recognize where they are in their journey immediately. The five-stage version is the customer-facing resolution; the seven-stage version is the internal operating resolution used to design what actually happens *within* each stage. Neither replaces the other — see `docs/venture-operating-system.md` for how this relates to the separate internal *production* pipeline (what Merchantiva's team does, as opposed to what the customer needs).
